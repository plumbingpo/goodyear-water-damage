Leaking Pipe Water Damage in Goodyear: What to Know

Most pipe leaks start without any warning. The drip is hidden in a wall cavity, a sink cabinet or beneath the slab, so the first clue tends to be a stain, a spongy patch of floor or an oddly high water bill. Often that means weeks of moisture have already gone into whatever surrounds the pipe. For Goodyear homes, our crew maps where the water traveled and dries the hidden damage along with the visible damage.

A plumber can stop the drip, though the water that soaked into the structure is still there afterward. This is where our team comes in. Our crew maps the damp materials with thermal imaging and moisture meters, opens up no more than necessary, and runs dehumidifiers and air movers until the readings return to normal. Mold often gets a head start during a long leak, which is why we look for it and treat the surfaces involved.

Right Now

What to Do Right Now

  1. Stop the Water SupplyFor a leak at a toilet or sink, turn off the small supply valve feeding that fixture. If you cannot find the source, turn off the main shutoff valve for the house.
  2. Keep Power AwayIf water is near outlets, switches or a light fixture, switch off the breaker for that area. Do not touch wet electrical devices or cords.
  3. Read the MeterShut off all taps and fixtures, then go look at the water meter. A dial that keeps turning points to a hidden pipe leak, which may be under the slab.
  4. Empty and PhotographEmpty the closet or cabinet by the leak, then photograph the pipe, any stains and any warped material before repairs start or items are discarded.
  5. Have the Area MeteredHave the area measured with meters before it is closed up. A wall that feels dry on the surface can still be wet inside the cavity.
Why It Happens

Why This Happens

  • A compression fitting or supply line connection at a sink or toilet that has loosened or worn with use.
  • A P-trap or drain connection under a sink that loosens and drips each time the basin empties.
  • A crimp ring or solder joint that gives out inside a ceiling or wall cavity where nobody can see it.
  • Water pressure set too high, which wears on every joint and valve until a slow weep begins.
  • Pipes in exterior walls or crawl spaces that crack after a freeze and seep once they thaw.
What Is at Risk

What the Water Damages

Ceiling and Drywall Damage

A pipe leaking above a ceiling or inside a wall leaves brown rings and bubbled paint. Wet drywall softens and sags over time, and those sections often have to come out.

Soaked Wall Insulation

Insulation in the wall soaks up leak water like a sponge and holds it against the framing. Since a closed wall gives it almost no airflow, soaked insulation is normally removed.

Decayed Wall Framing

Studs and the plate beneath them soften and decay when they sit wet for weeks. Wood that has lost its strength has to be cut out and replaced.

Flooring Damage

Moisture from a leaking slab or wall line spreads out below the finished floor. Hardwood cups, laminate swells at the seams, and adhesive under vinyl and tile lets go.

Insurance

What About Insurance?

A slow leak and a sudden one are not handled the same way by insurance. Sudden and accidental damage is often covered, but damage from a leak that went on for weeks or months usually is not, and some policies exclude it by name. An adjuster may look more kindly on a leak hidden from view than on one that could be seen and was left alone. Check your policy and ask your agent. We document the source of the leak, moisture readings and photos so the adjuster has clear facts.

What It Costs

What Drives the Price

  • Where the leak is, since an open cabinet costs less than a wall or slab.
  • How long the pipe leaked and how far the moisture spread from it.
  • How much drywall, insulation, cabinetry or flooring has to be removed and replaced.
  • How many rooms or levels the water reached, including a ceiling below the leak.
