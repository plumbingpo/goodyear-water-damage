Structural drying is the most important phase of water damage restoration, and the one untrained responders most often get wrong. After extraction removes the standing water, significant moisture remains trapped in structural materials, including drywall, wood framing, subflooring, concrete, insulation and other building components. This trapped moisture must be scientifically removed through controlled evaporation and dehumidification to prevent the mold growth, wood rot, material degradation and odor problems that develop when structures remain wet.

Psychrometry is the science of how air temperature, humidity and moisture interact, and the IICRC-certified technicians who dry structures for Goodyear Restoration know it well. For each job they apply it to calculate airflow patterns, temperature settings and the placement of equipment that will give the best conditions for evaporation. Different materials (drywall, hardwood, concrete, plaster) dry at different rates and need different approaches to reach acceptable moisture levels without causing additional damage.

Our structural drying process includes daily moisture monitoring with calibrated moisture meters, thermo-hygrometers and thermal imaging cameras. The result is a detailed log of readings that tracks how moisture comes down over time in each material that got wet. Those readings guide daily equipment adjustments that keep the drying as efficient as it can be. A surface that feels dry to your hand proves nothing, so the job is finished only once all affected materials test within the acceptable standards set by IICRC S500.

Why Hire a Professional for Structural Drying?

There is real science behind structural drying, which is why guessing does not work. Simply placing fans and hoping for the best is the most common cause of secondary mold damage after water events. To do it professionally, you have to know how each material takes on and gives up moisture, work out the grain depression, meaning the moisture removal capacity, for the job, and check progress every day. Without this knowledge, under-drying leaves moisture that feeds mold, while over-drying can damage wood and other materials.

Professional drying equipment is not a bigger version of what stores sell, it is a different kind of machine. High-velocity air movers produce precise airflow patterns that speed up evaporation from the structure, and low-grain refrigerant (LGR) commercial dehumidifiers capture 10 to 20 times the moisture a home unit can. Machines like these cost a great deal and take training to use well, which explains why professional results are so much better than a do-it-yourself effort.

Structural Drying Prevention Tips

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Address any water intrusion immediately, because the faster drying begins, the better the outcome
  • Keep belongings off the machines and out of the path of the air movers
  • Go by the meter, not by touch, since a dry-feeling surface may still hold moisture deep inside
  • Dehumidifiers lose efficiency when outside air gets in, so keep drying areas closed up
  • Plan for daily access so readings can be taken and equipment adjusted
  • Do not turn off professional drying equipment to reduce noise, because every hour matters

Warning Signs for Structural Drying

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • No visible water left, yet walls or floors still feel cold and damp
  • Wall finishes that start to peel or bubble in the days after water damage
  • Hardwood floors that are cupping, crowning or showing gaps between boards
  • Swollen trim and baseboards separating at the seams
  • After water damage, a meter shows elevated moisture in the floor, wall or ceiling
  • Rooms that stay humid even with windows open and fans running
  • Beads of moisture on windows and cold surfaces after the water event
